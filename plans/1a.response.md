**Resolved Design**

- The UI term is **Publication**. A recipe has at most one primary publication; no primary publication means it lives in the permanent Recipe Tin.
- Recipes can have a bottom-of-card **References** section with three kinds of entries: an in-app publication, an external URL, or a printed citation.
- Ingredient and amount are one underlying row, displayed in separate visual columns.
- Each ingredient row should have:
  - Ingredient name
  - Details: “wheat,” “Indian,” “Brummel & Brown”
  - Quantity: volume and/or weight
  - **Preparation**: diced, melted, crushed, room-temperature, divided, etc.
- “Preparation” is the right name for that third column. It can be a free-text field initially; making it a shared picklist later is easy.
- Food type is “what the dish is”; recipe type/meal type is “when or how it is used.” Both are useful, and both should support multiple values where appropriate.
- Cuisine is multi-select.
- Time categories are separate: chill, freeze, marinate, prep, mix, cook, bake, cool, rest, and total.
- The calculate icon can sum entered durations into total time. It should explicitly overwrite/update the total rather than silently treating it as authoritative.

**Scaling And Measurements**

For servings, store a baseline yield such as `Serves 4` or `Makes 24 cookies`. The view control can offer:

- `Servings`: enter the desired serving count; the app calculates the multiplier.
- `Multiply`: enter $n$ to use $n\times$ the recipe.
- `Divide`: enter $n$ to use $1/n$ the recipe.

That gives the user the no-math servings path while retaining the manual controls. Recipes that yield discrete items should support both “servings” and “makes N items.”

Each ingredient row can have side-by-side volume and weight inputs. In view mode:

- Only one available: `1 cup`
- Both available: `1 cup / 254 g`

The app scales both stored values directly. It should offer within-dimension conversions, such as cups to tablespoons or grams to ounces. It should not auto-convert volume to weight unless a trusted ingredient density exists.

**Ingredient Sorting**

Treat the entered ingredient order as canonical editing order. Sorting is a display preference and never reshuffles the recipe itself.

Support:

- Appears in instructions: default.
- Alphabetical: ascending/descending.
- Unit size: cup, tablespoon, teaspoon, etc., ascending/descending.
- Order entered.

“Appears in instructions” needs a lightweight link between an ingredient and the first instruction step that uses it. This can be maintained by the recipe editor, rather than trying to infer ingredients from arbitrary prose. For a row with both volume and weight, unit-size sorting should use the currently selected display dimension.

**One Important Refinement**

“Specific occasion” and “occasionally” are different kinds of information:

- `Verdict` should describe the outcome: favorite, delicious, needs work, no, etc.
- `Cooking cadence` should describe whether it belongs in the regular rotation, is occasional, or is for a specific occasion.
- `Occasion details` appears only when the cadence is “specific occasion.”

That avoids putting frequency and emotional judgment into the same picklist, while preserving the feeling you want.

**Necessary Remaining Clarifications**

1. For a magazine, does a Publication represent the magazine generally, or a specific issue? I recommend general publication plus optional `issue/edition/date` on the recipe’s source locator.

2. For a book, do you need edition and ISBN fields now? They are cheap to support, even if optional.

3. Do public recipe links default to enabled, or does each recipe have a Share toggle? Either way, use a long random public ID, not a sequential ID, and allow links to be revoked.

4. Should a shared recipe expose its publication/reference links, or show only the recipe content and print/scale controls? I recommend showing publication attribution but keeping all app navigation hidden.

5. What is the export promise? I recommend a ZIP containing JSON for complete re-import, CSV exports for spreadsheet use, and an `images/` folder. A plain CSV cannot faithfully represent steps, references, relationships, and multi-value fields.

6. Can a Recipe Tin recipe later be assigned to a publication, or is Tin membership permanent for originals? I recommend allowing assignment later, while preserving its original/reference history.

**Storage And Accounts**

Use **Supabase Storage** for covers and recipe images, not database blobs and not a local server data folder. Store files in private buckets organized by account/recipe, with database rows holding paths and metadata. That works naturally with Vercel and supports export.

Google sign-in is the cleanest initial account path. Password sign-in without email verification is technically possible, but mistyped addresses cannot recover their accounts and anyone can claim any address. For a personal/private app, either Google-only initially or password accounts with email verification is the less troublesome foundation.

Accessibility should be staged, but do not defer the basics: semantic labels, keyboard operation, focus visibility, and adequate contrast belong in the first release. Full screen-reader optimization can mature later; inaccessible form controls are expensive to unwind afterward.
