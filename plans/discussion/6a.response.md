`Kind` is pleasantly short, but I would not use it for this column. It fits “all-purpose flour,” but not “large garlic” or “14 oz can.” Those are size and package specifications, not kinds.

I’d label the column **Detail**. It is broad without being vague, and your table works naturally:

| Ingredient | Quantity | Unit | Detail | Preparation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| flour | 1 | cup | all-purpose | |
| garlic | 2–3 | cloves | large | minced |
| salt | | to taste | | |
| coconut milk | 1 | can | 14 oz | |

The rendered rows become:

- `1 cup all-purpose flour`
- `2–3 large cloves garlic, minced`
- `salt, to taste`
- `1 14 oz can coconut milk`

One implementation adjustment: `to taste` should be an **unmeasured quantity mode**, not a normal unit. The editor’s type selector can be `Volume`, `Weight`, `Count`, or `Unmeasured`. Choosing Unmeasured offers approved phrases such as `to taste`, `as needed`, and `for garnish`.

For coconut milk, selecting Count → `can` can reveal the optional Detail field, where `14 oz` goes. It stays readable without treating “14 oz can” as a custom, non-convertible unit.
